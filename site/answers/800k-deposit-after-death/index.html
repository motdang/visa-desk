Retirement visa · Non-O · Thai banks

Your ฿800,000 deposit, when you die.

Thai banks have no payable-on-death (POD) or transfer-on-death (TOD) beneficiary on a deposit account. On your death the account freezes, and the bank pays the estate administrator (ผู้จัดการมรดก, the person a Thai court puts in charge of your estate). A Thai will chooses that person. It does not skip the court.

The will

Thai Civil and Commercial Code, sections as numbered.

Without one

For a bank deposit, the law of the place you were domiciled decides who inherits (Conflict of Laws Act B.E. 2481, s.38). Where that is Thailand, a spouse, children and living parents share it (s.1629, s.1630, s.1635); with none of them, brothers and sisters, then half brothers and sisters, grandparents, uncles and aunts. A spouse you have separated from is a spouse until the divorce is registered.

The executor

Your choice, named in the will (s.1712), with a second in case the first cannot act. The court appoints the person named unless they are a minor, legally incapacitated or bankrupt (s.1718). Any nationality. Someone who can come to Thailand, or will hire a Thai lawyer, makes it quicker.

Signing

Written, dated, signed by you in front of two witnesses present together, who sign too (s.1656). A witness, or a witness's spouse, who is left something loses that gift (s.1653). Pick witnesses who inherit nothing.

Two wills, not one

A Thai court can work from a US or other foreign will, translated and authenticated, and the bank still waits for the Thai court. A Thai will for Thai assets is the shorter road. Each will names its own assets and leaves the other standing.

Questions

Do Thai banks have payable-on-death (POD) or transfer-on-death (TOD) accounts?

Not on deposit accounts. When the bank learns of the death it freezes the account and pays the estate administrator a Thai court appoints, on the court order and its certificate of finality.

Can my heirs withdraw my retirement deposit with my death certificate?

Not at ฿400,000 to ฿800,000. Some branches pay small balances to heirs without a court order, reported at ฿50,000 to ฿200,000; that is the branch's choice and no published rule. Above it, the bank waits for a court order.

Is my US will enough for my Thai bank account?

A Thai court can work from a foreign will, but it has to be translated and authenticated, and the bank still waits for a Thai court order. A second will, in Thai and English, for Thai assets only, is the shorter road. Each will says it does not revoke the other.

Can I choose my own administrator or executor in Thailand?

Yes. A will can name the executor (Civil and Commercial Code s.1712). The court then appoints that person unless they are a minor, legally incapacitated or bankrupt (s.1718). They need not be Thai.

Who gets my Thai bank money if I die without a will?

For a bank deposit, the law of the place you were domiciled decides (Conflict of Laws Act B.E. 2481, s.38). If that is Thailand, a spouse, children and living parents share it (Civil and Commercial Code s.1629, s.1630, s.1635); with none of them, brothers and sisters, then half brothers and sisters, grandparents, uncles and aunts. A registered spouse is a spouse until the divorce is registered.

How long does probate take in Thailand, and what does it cost?

Four to eight months in most uncontested cases. Court fees ฿200 to ฿2,000; lawyers' fees quoted ฿50,000 to ฿200,000; certified translation per page on top (Expat Tax Thailand, 2026).

Does a joint account avoid probate in Thailand?

Usually not. Most joint accounts freeze on the death of one holder and wait for the court order too.

Does the whole ฿800,000 have to stay in Thailand?

For the deposit route, ฿800,000 sits in a Thai bank in your name for 2 months before you apply and 3 months after the extension, and stays at ฿400,000 or more for the rest of the year. Income of ฿65,000 a month, or a mix of income and deposit, are the other two routes.

Who can witness a Thai will?

Two adults, present together while you sign. A witness, or a witness's spouse, who is left something in the will loses that gift (Civil and Commercial Code s.1653), so pick witnesses who inherit nothing.
